STACS · 2025 – NOW

Energy-aware cache replacement

Prof. Joshua San Miguel · STACS Lab

Cache replacement policies are usually judged by hit rate alone, but not every insertion that helps hit rate is worth its energy cost. I am building simulation tooling that models cache access sequences under different replacement policies (LRU, FIFO, Belady's MIN) alongside an exhaustive oracle, validating each policy against brute-force ground truth across enumerated access sequences to identify replacements a policy makes without a real benefit to hit rate. So far, that analysis shows Belady's MIN, while optimal for hit rate, makes about 30% more evictions than necessary, with roughly one in ten sequences showing avoidable churn. The direction I am working toward is a replacement policy that targets eviction count directly, reducing that churn while keeping hit rate optimal.

EDGE-LLM · 2024 – NOW

Energy-efficient LLM inference on edge hardware

Prof. Setareh Behroozi · ECE

Large language models are typically quantized with one precision setting applied uniformly across the whole model, but different layers and operations tolerate lower precision very differently. I am building a selective bit-usage framework that splits model weights into fast-memory MSBs and on-demand LSBs, guided by saliency rather than fetched uniformly, to cut memory and compute cost for on-device inference. Saliency-guided bit masking on LLaMA-7B holds up substantially better than random masking as compression gets more aggressive, and I'm now extending evaluation to accuracy benchmarks and running an oracle-gap analysis to see how much headroom is left for allocating precision dynamically, per token, at runtime.
